# Source Corpus and Provenance

This skill was distilled from a local analysis of 20 Discussion or Discussion-like sections selected from a 747-PDF research library. The analyzed corpus contains approximately 24,300 English words and 913 sentences.

## Journal coverage

- Nature
- Science
- Nature Geoscience
- Nature Communications
- Science Advances
- Geophysical Research Letters
- Geology
- Reviews of Geophysics

The corpus emphasizes induced seismicity, fluid–fault interaction, pore pressure, stress transfer, earthquake swarms, landslide–earthquake cascades, and hazard implications. It also includes review-style conclusions and one cross-domain Discussion to identify patterns that generalize beyond a single subfield.

## Discussion forms represented

- explicit standalone Discussion;
- combined Discussion and Conclusions;
- Implications section;
- review Summary or Conclusions;
- Nature/Science-style interpretation integrated into the latter part of Results.

## Corpus-level observations

The most frequent functional signals were uncertainty calibration, interpretation, and causal mechanism, followed by contrast, implication, future work, direct result reference, limitation, and explicit literature dialogue. `however`, `although`, and `consistent with` were recurrent, but their value came from the logical work they performed rather than frequency alone.

The most transferable architecture was:

> key finding -> mechanism -> literature or independent evidence -> uncertainty/alternative -> applicability boundary -> theory or risk implication -> testable next step

Top-journal prose in the corpus was characterized by controlled causal language, explicit boundaries, and a concrete payoff. It was not characterized by ornate vocabulary.

## Provenance note

The source library and raw extracts are not distributed with this public skill. This reference records only the high-level provenance and transferable writing principles.
