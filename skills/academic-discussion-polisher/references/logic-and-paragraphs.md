# Discussion Logic and Paragraph Architecture

Use this reference for full sections, major restructuring, or logic diagnosis.

## Core reasoning chain

A strong research Discussion usually follows this reasoning, with compression or reordering when the genre requires it:

> key finding -> mechanism -> independent support or literature comparison -> alternative explanation -> uncertainty and scope -> theoretical or practical implication -> testable next step

This is a reasoning chain, not a mandatory set of headings. Nature- and Science-style reports may embed it in the latter part of Results. Short field-journal papers may combine mechanism, limitation, and conclusion in a few paragraphs.

## Six-paragraph default for original research

### 1. Key finding and interpretive problem

Select one to three findings that determine the paper's contribution. State why they are surprising, consequential, or not explained by the prevailing model. Do not recap every figure.

### 2. Preferred mechanism

Build the physical or conceptual chain explicitly:

> observed pattern -> intermediate process -> final response -> conditions required

Check that the mechanism explains the magnitude, timing, spatial distribution, or sign of the effect rather than merely naming a plausible process.

### 3. Independent evidence and literature dialogue

Compare the inference with an independent observation, model, experiment, or published constraint. Organize literature by relationship: agreement, partial agreement, conflict, alternative mechanism, or applicability boundary. Avoid author-by-author lists.

### 4. Alternatives, robustness, and limitations

Address the strongest plausible alternative. For models or inversions, separate absolute values sensitive to parameters, spatial or temporal patterns that remain stable, and mechanism conclusions that require independent validation.

A useful limitation has three parts: the limitation, the affected inference, and the status of the main conclusion.

### 5. Theoretical or methodological significance

State precisely which assumption, mechanism, interpretation, or modeling practice changes. Do not use “important implications” without naming the changed quantity, model, or decision.

### 6. Hazard, management, or testable prediction

For applied work, connect the mechanism to a metric, time window, spatial domain, monitoring strategy, or operational decision. End with a prediction that can be tested using specified data or observations.

## Paragraph microstructure

Each paragraph should normally contain:

1. **Topic:** the fact or question addressed.
2. **Evidence:** data, figure, magnitude, trend, or external constraint.
3. **Interpretation:** the mechanism or scientific judgment.
4. **Boundary/payoff:** alternative, scope condition, or implication.

Evidence without interpretation reads like Results. Interpretation without evidence reads as speculation. Implication without the preceding chain reads as overstatement.

## Common organizational variants

### Competing-mechanism Discussion

Use when several processes may operate: define the observations every mechanism must explain, assess each mechanism, state the conditions controlling relative importance, and end with a discriminating prediction.

### Model-robustness Discussion

Use for simulations, inversions, or statistical models: key pattern -> parameter and boundary tests -> robust versus parameter-dependent features -> independent comparison -> missing processes -> implication.

### Multi-evidence convergence

Use for combined seismic, geodetic, geological, or geochemical evidence: phenomenon -> evidence stream A -> evidence stream B -> agreement/conflict -> unified model and prediction.

### Risk-action Implications

Use for short applied conclusions: revealed risk -> exposure or consequence -> concrete action -> unresolved warning or prediction problem.

## Diagnostic questions

- Does the first paragraph identify an interpretive problem rather than repeat the abstract?
- Can each paragraph be labeled with one primary function?
- Does every mechanism claim have evidence and required conditions?
- Is the strongest alternative addressed fairly?
- Are uncertainty in amplitude and uncertainty in mechanism distinguished?
- Does the conclusion move beyond summary without exceeding the evidence?
- Is future work stated as a test rather than a wish?
