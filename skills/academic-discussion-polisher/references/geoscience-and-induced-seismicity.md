# Geoscience and Induced-Seismicity Discussion Guide

Use this reference for induced earthquakes, reservoir seismicity, fluid injection, geothermal stimulation, pore-pressure or poroelastic modeling, Coulomb stress, fault stability, and related geoscience Discussions.

## Separate three epistemic levels

### Direct output or observation

Examples: pore pressure, displacement, stress, Coulomb failure stress, migration rate, hypocenter distribution, focal mechanism, velocity anomaly.

Use: `shows`, `is observed`, `the model predicts`, `the inversion yields`.

### Mechanism inference

Examples: diffusion dominates migration; poroelastic stress transfer activates a remote fault; aseismic slip transmits stress.

Use: `suggests`, `indicates`, `is consistent with`, `is best explained by`.

### Event attribution

Examples: an operation induced or triggered a specific earthquake.

Require converging evidence and normally use `likely induced`, `likely triggered`, or `supports an induced origin`. Avoid an unqualified causal statement based on timing alone.

## Forward-model–observation loop

Organize the interpretation around:

1. predicted pore-pressure, stress, Coulomb stress, deformation, or migration pattern;
2. observed earthquake timing and spatial migration;
3. consistency with fault geometry and focal mechanisms;
4. independent constraints from velocity structure, Vp/Vs, resistivity, geochemistry, InSAR, GNSS, water level, or injection history;
5. mismatched regions or times and the missing process they may indicate;
6. an observation that would discriminate among diffusion, poroelasticity, aseismic slip, and earthquake-to-earthquake transfer.

## Model robustness

Discuss three levels separately:

- **Amplitude:** often sensitive to permeability, storage, friction, initial stress, elastic properties, source history, and boundaries.
- **Pattern:** high-value zones, migration direction, sign, and relative timing may remain robust.
- **Mechanism:** requires parameter tests, competing models, and independent observations.

Do not write `the model is robust` without specifying the level.

## Competing fluid-related mechanisms

When relevant, distinguish direct pore-pressure diffusion, poroelastic stress transfer, aseismic slip, permeability enhancement or compartmentalization, static or dynamic earthquake triggering, background tectonic loading, and natural deep-fluid upwelling.

State which observations each mechanism explains and which it does not. A multi-process explanation is acceptable when relative contributions are tied to distance, time, connectivity, or stress state.

## Causal-attribution checklist

Assess:

- temporal response compatible with the candidate mechanism;
- spatial relation among operation/reservoir, perturbation, and fault;
- sufficient and correctly signed stress or effective-stress change;
- favorable fault orientation and proximity to failure;
- migration or delay consistent with a propagation mechanism;
- natural seismicity and tectonic alternatives;
- independent geological, geophysical, or geochemical evidence.

Grade separate events independently when confidence differs.

## Hazard and management implications

Tie implications to a specific quantity or decision: maximum magnitude, exceedance probability, response delay, mitigation time window, remote triggering, traffic-light threshold, monitoring geometry, injection schedule, reservoir siting, or infrastructure exposure.

Do not imply that laboratory or site-specific findings provide an operational guarantee. Prefer `preliminary evidence of feasibility`, `may inform`, or `should be evaluated under site-specific conditions` when scale-up is untested.

## Preferred terminology

- `pore-pressure diffusion/perturbation/buildup/dissipation`
- `poroelastic stress transfer/coupling/response`
- `Coulomb stress change/stressing rate/stress redistribution`
- `fault reactivation/stability/susceptibility/orientation`
- `critically stressed fault/pre-existing fault/permeable fault zone`
- `spatiotemporal migration/clustering/evolution`
- `hydraulic diffusivity/permeability enhancement/pressure front`
- `model sensitivity/parameter uncertainty/boundary condition`
- `independent constraint/converging evidence/alternative explanation`
- `triggering mechanism/causal attribution/operational control`
- `seismic hazard/maximum magnitude/exceedance probability`

Before finalizing, verify that the Discussion does not equate Coulomb stress increase with inevitable failure, spatial coincidence with hydraulic connection, modeled diffusion with observed fluid movement, an assumed initial stress field with observation, a laboratory strategy with field-scale safety, or timing alone with induced causation.
