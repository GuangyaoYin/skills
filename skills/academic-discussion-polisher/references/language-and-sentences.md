# Discussion Language and Sentence Patterns

Use this reference for sentence-level polishing, claim calibration, transitions, and reusable constructions.

## Claim-strength ladder

Approximate progression from weak compatibility to strong direct support:

`is compatible with` < `may reflect` < `suggests` < `indicates` < `supports` < `shows` < `demonstrates`

Choose by evidence, not by stylistic preference.

- `may/might/could`: possible mechanism, outcome, or generalization.
- `appears/seems`: visible trend with limited resolution or sample size.
- `likely/most likely`: preferred explanation after comparison.
- `suggests`: inference not uniquely required by the evidence.
- `indicates`: converging evidence points to the interpretation.
- `supports`: evidence strengthens an explicit hypothesis.
- `shows`: direct result within the tested scope.
- `demonstrates`: strong direct relationship with plausible alternatives addressed.

## Finding to mechanism

- `The observed [pattern] cannot be explained by [simple mechanism] alone.`
- `This pattern is most readily explained by [mechanism], which [physical process].`
- `We attribute the [increase/decrease] in [Y] to [X], because [reason].`
- `Under [condition], [X] promotes [Y] by [mechanism].`
- `[X] leads to [Y], which in turn [secondary effect].`
- `Rather than reflecting [A], the result is more consistent with [B].`

## Multiple or competing mechanisms

- `[A] explains [subset], whereas [B] is required to account for [other subset].`
- `Both [A] and [B] may contribute, but their relative importance varies with [condition].`
- `Although [A] remains possible, it does not explain [critical observation].`
- `A combined mechanism involving [A] and [B] provides the most coherent explanation for [evidence set].`
- `The present data cannot distinguish uniquely between [A] and [B]; however, [independent evidence] favors [A].`
- `The mechanisms predict different [observables], providing a direct test.`

## Literature dialogue

- `This interpretation is consistent with [specific observation] reported by [study].`
- `The inference is further supported by [independent evidence].`
- `Our estimate agrees with [external estimate] within [uncertainty].`
- `In contrast to [study], we find [difference], likely because [defined reason].`
- `Our results extend previous work by showing that [new scope].`
- `The mechanism may reconcile [observation A] with [observation B].`

Do not write an empty `This is consistent with previous studies.` Name the point of agreement.

## Robustness and limitations

- `Varying [parameter] changes the absolute magnitude of [Y] but preserves its spatial pattern.`
- `The first-order trend is robust across the tested range of [parameter].`
- `Because [parameter] is poorly constrained, we interpret [quantity] as an upper/lower bound.`
- `The model reproduces [A] but underestimates [B], suggesting that [missing process] may be important.`
- `This does not imply that [overinterpretation].`
- `Because [variable] was held constant, its influence cannot be evaluated here.`
- `The present data cannot distinguish between [A] and [B].`
- `This limitation affects [secondary inference] but not [robust primary result].`

## Causal attribution

- `Temporal association alone is insufficient to establish causation.`
- `Attribution is supported by the combined evidence from [timing], [location], [stress], and [geometry].`
- `The event is best described as likely induced/triggered rather than definitively caused by [operation].`
- `[Operation] may have advanced failure that would otherwise have occurred later.`
- `The perturbation is sufficient to reactivate favorably oriented faults close to failure.`
- `Subsequent events may reflect earthquake-to-earthquake stress transfer rather than forcing by [operation] alone.`

## Implications and future work

- `These findings provide a physical explanation for [previously unresolved observation].`
- `The inferred mechanism links [small-scale process] to [field-scale behavior].`
- `Accounting for [process] may change estimates of [hazard metric].`
- `Identifying [indicator] can guide [monitoring or operational] decisions.`
- `Future observations should test whether [prediction] occurs under [condition].`
- `Distinguishing between [A] and [B] will require [specific data or method].`
- `The proposed mechanism predicts [observable], which can be evaluated using [dataset].`

## Transitions with real logical jobs

- **however:** a result, limitation, or alternative that changes the interpretation.
- **although/despite:** concede a constraint while stating what remains supported.
- **whereas/in contrast:** compare defined objects or trends.
- **therefore/thus:** draw a conclusion already licensed by the prior evidence.
- **on the other hand:** introduce a genuine alternative consequence or branch; use sparingly.
- **taken together:** combine multiple independent findings, not repeated versions of one result.

Avoid or justify `prove`, `clearly`, `obviously`, `very`, `extremely`, unsupported `significantly`, `perfectly consistent`, `the reason is`, and unverified `for the first time`.
