# Chinese-English Translation and Polishing Decisions

Use this reference for Chinese-to-English translation, bilingual revision, or substantial English polishing.

## Preserve scientific responsibility

Before translating a sentence, identify who or what performs the action, whether the source states observation, inference, causation, or possibility, which clause supplies evidence, and what uncertainty or applicability boundary applies.

Do not strengthen “可能表明”“可能与……有关”“支持……的解释” into `demonstrates`, `is caused by`, or an unqualified fact.

## Translate logic, not word order

Chinese academic prose often places background, conditions, and several causal steps before the main claim. In English:

1. put the topic or principal finding early;
2. keep the subject visible across sentences;
3. split a sentence when it contains more than one major logical turn;
4. place conditions next to the claim they limit;
5. avoid abstract-noun chains when a clear verb is available.

Preferred transformation:

> Chinese order: condition + observation + possible reason + implication  
> English order: observation + interpretation + condition/boundary + implication

## Common scientific mappings

- “结果表明” -> `show`, `indicate`, or `suggest` according to evidence strength.
- “可能是由于” -> `This may be due to ...` or `A possible explanation is ...`.
- “与……一致” -> `is consistent with ...`; name the exact agreement.
- “进一步说明” -> `further supports/indicates ...`, not automatically `demonstrates`.
- “不可忽略” -> explain the measurable contribution instead of `cannot be ignored`.
- “具有重要意义” -> specify the model, hazard estimate, monitoring practice, or decision affected.
- “仍需进一步研究” -> state what observation could distinguish the alternatives.
- “在一定程度上” -> replace with a concrete scope or remove if empty.
- “值得注意的是” -> lead with the fact; use `Notably` only when justified.

## Tense and voice

- Use past tense for procedures and specific observations made in the study.
- Use present tense for figures/tables, established interpretations, and currently valid conclusions.
- Use present perfect for a continuing research trend when needed, but do not overuse it.
- Prefer active voice when the actor matters: `We tested`, `The model predicts`, `The data indicate`.
- Use passive voice when the process or object matters more than the actor.

## Control long sentences

Split when a sentence contains two independent contrasts; evidence, mechanism, limitation, and implication together; more than one ambiguous `which`; a long subject separated from its verb; or citations attached to different claims.

Retain a long sentence when its clauses form one transparent causal chain and every modifier has a clear attachment.

## Cohesion and terminology

Do not begin every sentence with `This`. Use a specific noun phrase, a mechanism subject, a scoped condition, or a synthesis phrase. Repeat technical terms when synonyms would create ambiguity; terminological stability matters more than stylistic variety.

## Ambiguity and missing support

If the Chinese text does not identify whether a statement is observed or inferred, choose the conservative English form and flag the ambiguity. Resolve pronouns only when context is clear.

Do not silently repair missing citations, absent parameter tests, or a broken causal step by inventing content. Improve the sentence around the gap and list the missing support after the revised text.

## Polishing priority

1. scientific meaning and causal strength;
2. paragraph logic and sentence order;
3. subject and modifier clarity;
4. concision and removal of repetition;
5. grammar, articles, prepositions, and punctuation;
6. rhythm and stylistic refinement.

Never sacrifice the first two priorities for smoother English.
