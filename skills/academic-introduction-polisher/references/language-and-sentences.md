# Language and Sentence Bank

Use these as rhetorical patterns, not text to copy mechanically. Replace every placeholder and preserve the actual evidence level.

## Established knowledge

- `[Observed pattern] is generally interpreted as evidence for [mechanism].`
- `It has been well documented that [established finding].`
- `Previous studies have shown that [finding].`
- `Several mechanisms have been proposed to explain [phenomenon], including [A], [B], and [C].`
- `The predominant view is that [mechanism] controls [outcome].`

## Contrast and gap

- `However, this interpretation cannot readily explain [observation].`
- `Although [fact A] is well established, [question B] remains unclear.`
- `Despite considerable progress, [variable] remains poorly constrained.`
- `Existing evidence is largely indirect, leaving [relationship] ill-constrained.`
- `To date, studies have focused mainly on [A], while [B] has received less attention.`
- `Most previous studies assume that [A] dominates and therefore neglect [B].`
- `The relative contributions of [A] and [B] remain unresolved.`
- `Previous studies have reached contrasting conclusions, partly because they differ in [assumptions].`

## Study opportunity and action

- `A well-monitored [event/experiment] provides a unique opportunity to investigate [question].`
- `Because [dataset] directly constrains [unknown quantity], it allows us to distinguish between [A] and [B].`
- `The combination of [dataset A] and [dataset B] enables an independent test of [mechanism].`
- `Here we use [data/method] to quantify [target process].`
- `In this study, we apply [method] to [dataset] to estimate [unknown variable].`
- `We combine [observation], [inversion], and [forward modeling] to test whether [mechanism] can account for [pattern].`

## Results and viewpoints previewed in an Introduction

- `Further investigation indicates that [deeper pattern or interpretation].`
- `Our results show that [directly supported finding].`
- `The results reveal that [previously unrecognized pattern or relationship].`
- `Our observations delineate [structure, distribution, boundary, or migration path], highlighting [mechanism or significance].`
- `The observations support a model in which [causal sequence].`
- `These findings challenge the common assumption that [assumption].`
- `We argue that [conceptual claim].`
- `We propose that [interaction] should be treated as [new framing].`

## Claim-strength ladder

This ordering is approximate and must be adjusted to context:

`may/might/could` → `suggest` → `indicate` → `show` → `demonstrate`

- `suggest`: indirect, preliminary, or non-unique evidence;
- `indicate`: convergent evidence supporting an interpretation;
- `show`: direct, robust result;
- `demonstrate`: strong test with alternatives substantially excluded;
- `reveal`: emphasizes discovery or a previously hidden pattern, not necessarily stronger proof;
- `delineate`: maps or bounds a spatial/structural feature;
- `support/is consistent with`: favors a model without proving uniqueness;
- `argue/propose`: conceptual or interpretive contribution.

Do not upgrade verbs merely to sound more authoritative.

## Questions

Avoid direct questions in ordinary research Introductions.

Instead of:

`What controls the delayed seismic response?`

Use a gap:

`The process controlling the delayed seismic response remains unclear.`

Or an objective:

`Here we investigate the process controlling the delayed seismic response.`

A single rhetorical question may be retained only when the requested genre is deliberately compressed and the question creates a precise, memorable transition. Do not use multiple rhetorical questions.

## Cohesion patterns

- A contrast word must connect opposing propositions, not merely new topics.
- Use parallel syntax for competing mechanisms or settings.
- Put known information early and the new information near the sentence end.
- Use short sentences for the central gap or claim; use longer sentences for qualifications and evidence integration.
- Prefer concrete verbs such as `constrain`, `quantify`, `distinguish`, `delineate`, and `reconcile` over vague verbs such as `explore` when the actual operation is known.
