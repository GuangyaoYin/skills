# Translation and Polishing Guide

## Meaning-first translation

Translate the scientific relationship, not the Chinese surface order. Before writing English, identify:

- the main claim;
- evidence versus interpretation;
- causal, temporal, spatial, and contrast relationships;
- uncertainty and scope;
- the sentence's rhetorical role in the paragraph.

Rebuild the sentence in idiomatic academic English while preserving these elements.

## Preserve scientific boundaries

- Do not turn correlation into causation.
- Do not turn `可能` into a definite claim.
- Do not convert an interpretation into a measured result.
- Preserve numerical values, units, directions, signs, time intervals, and comparative baselines.
- Preserve citation ownership: distinguish the author's result from previous studies.
- Keep established technical terms consistent across the Introduction.

## Preferred translation choices

- `研究表明`: choose among `previous studies have shown`, `our results show`, `evidence suggests`, or `analysis indicates` according to source and strength.
- `进一步研究表明`: use `Further investigation indicates that ...` when a deeper follow-up analysis supports the statement.
- `结果揭示`: use `the results reveal ...` only for a newly exposed pattern or relationship; otherwise use `show` or `indicate`.
- `观测刻画了`: use `our observations delineate ...` for spatial structures, boundaries, distributions, or migration paths.
- `尚不清楚`: prefer `remains unclear`, followed by the exact unknown.
- `研究较少`: rewrite as the specific neglected process, variable, scale, or evidence limitation.
- `具有重要意义`: specify the consequence: `has implications for [mechanism, monitoring, forecasting, or assessment]`.

## Polish at three levels

### Logic

Repair paragraph order, missing premises, false contrasts, abrupt gaps, and mismatches between the gap and study design.

### Sentence

Repair overloaded sentences, dangling modifiers, unclear antecedents, broken parallelism, unnecessary nominalization, repeated subjects, and excessive passive voice.

### Word choice

Replace vague or inflated language with precise verbs and calibrated claims. Avoid decorative synonym replacement that changes technical meaning.

## Tense defaults

- Present tense for established knowledge and what the paper shows as a lasting claim.
- Present perfect for a body of prior research with current relevance.
- Past tense for specific prior studies, observations, or procedures.
- Present tense for the paper's purpose (`Here we investigate...`) and often for figure-independent principal claims (`Our results show...`), subject to journal convention.

## Output discipline

For full polishing, return a clean revised version before explanations. Keep comments concise and focus on consequential changes. When a Chinese sentence is scientifically ambiguous, give the most plausible translation and flag the ambiguity rather than inventing a resolution.

When the source is structurally weak, do not merely beautify individual sentences. Reorder or merge material when authorized, while preserving citations and marking any claim that requires author verification.
